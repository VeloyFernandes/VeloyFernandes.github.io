Buckling of corrugated panels

A corrugated sheet can resist buckling tens of times better than a flat one, or worse than the flat one. It depends on which way you push. This page is my 2023 MSc dissertation at Swansea University, rebuilt as a simulator and corrected: two finite element solvers run in your browser, so every number below is computed for the panel you choose.

Direction and geometry

Folding a sheet moves material away from the mid-plane, which stiffens it against bending along the folds. Across the folds the sheet can still bend like a flat strip, and there is more of it to bend, so it is softer than the flat plate it was made from. What I corrected

Rebuilding the work with independent solvers showed where the 2023 version was right and where it was not.

The conclusion on corrugation

The dissertation concluded that round corrugated panels resist buckling best. Its own numbers say otherwise for load across the corrugations: flat 47.1, trapezoidal 39.0, round 18.3 kN/m. Corrugation helps only along the folds, where it helps enormously.

Round corrugation stiffness

The MATLAB script had an operator-precedence slip (Rl/2*R for Rl/(2*R)), used the bending term D66 where the membrane term A66 belongs, and fed an already wrong A22 into D22. The corrected bending stiffness along a round corrugation is 4.44 MN·m, not 11.0 kN·m.

The Ansys results along the corrugations

For both corrugated panels the Ansys load along the folds was identical for pinned and fixed ends, and far below the theory. A finite strip model built here from plate theory agrees with the theory instead, so those four Ansys values most likely came from a boundary condition set-up that did not act on the mode, and should not be used.

Yield was missing

Along the folds the elastic buckling load of this thick panel is so high that the steel yields first. The round panel would need 36,800 kN/m to buckle but yields at 4,080 kN/m. The simulator now reports whichever limit comes first.

Units and data

The loads are line loads in N/m, not forces in N. Young's modulus was printed once as 21 GPa; the calculations used the correct 210 GPa.

What held up

The flat panel results, and the trapezoidal and round results across the corrugations, are confirmed here to within 0.2% of the homogenised theory. The Ansys round panel values sit 7% above, which is consistent with a coarse shell mesh on tight curves.

Method

Equivalent plate. The corrugated sheet is replaced by a flat orthotropic plate with the same stiffness, using the closed-form homogenisation of Xia, Friswell and Saavedra Flores (2012). This gives the bending stiffness across the folds, D₁₁ = (c/l)·D, and along them, D₂₂, from the shape of one corrugation.

Across the corrugations. A wide panel bends cylindrically, so a unit strip is a folded plane-strain beam. The page meshes the real profile with frame elements and solves the linear buckling eigenproblem. It does not use the equivalent plate, which makes it an independent check on it.

Along the corrugations. A semi-analytical finite strip model: every flat of the profile is a plate strip with membrane and bending action, and the buckle is a sine wave along the folds. It captures local buckling of the flats and global buckling of the panel in one model. Fixed ends use the effective length, half the panel length.

Yield and design load. Along the folds the section yields when the stress on the developed area reaches yield. Across them the sheet is bent by the offset of the thrust line, and first yield is reached much earlier. The design estimate combines buckling and yield with the EN 1993-1-1 column curve c, as an indication only.

Limits. Linear elastic eigenvalue buckling of a perfect panel under uniform end compression. No imperfections, no post-buckling, no shear deformation, isotropic sheet. Real panels fail below the eigenvalue; that is what the design estimate hints at.

Code. The same models are in Python, with tests and the corrected MATLAB script, in the GitHub repository. The Python and browser versions agree to four significant figures.
