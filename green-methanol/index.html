Why biogas CO₂?

Raw biogas is roughly 40 % CO₂. Upgrading it to biomethane already separates that CO₂ as a concentrated, biogenic stream — a ready carbon source that would otherwise be vented.

Why alkaline electrolysis?

Alkaline electrolysers are the most mature and lowest-cost water-splitting technology at the tens-of-megawatts scale, and they tolerate the steady, grid-backed operation assumed in this study.

Why wind and solar together?

In Denmark solar peaks in summer and wind in winter. Co-locating them flattens the seasonal profile and cuts the energy that has to be traded with the grid — see seasonal complementarity.

Solar farm

Modelled with pvlib: Sandia module and CEC inverter models from the SAM database, SAPM cell temperature, fixed tilt facing south. The string layout is sized from the inverter's voltage window and a 1.25 DC/AC ratio, then one inverter block is simulated at one-minute steps and scaled to the farm.

Method, data and limitations

The website re-runs the project notebook's models and publishes the results as static data; all scenario and cost calculations then happen in your browser.

  1. Solar simulation

    pvlib ModelChain for one inverter block (18 modules per string × 184 strings), Ineichen clear-sky irradiance at one-minute resolution, 20 °C ambient, AC output clipped at the inverter rating and scaled by 323 inverters, then averaged to hours.

  2. Wind simulation

    NASA POWER hourly wind speed, direction, temperature and pressure; power-law extrapolation to 130 m; windpowerlib TurbineClusterModelChain with barometric density, density-corrected and smoothed power curve, and Jensen wake model.

  3. Combined table

    Both series are aligned on an hourly Europe/Copenhagen index for 2023 and merged; gaps are treated as zero generation.

  4. Energy balance

    Constant demand of the electrolyser (53 MW) and methanol plant (2 MW) times a 92 % uptime factor is subtracted hour by hour. Surplus is exported, deficit imported.

  5. Site & orientation studies

    31 Danish locations are simulated with local all-sky irradiance and wind data; 703 tilt/azimuth combinations are simulated for the project site.

  6. Cost of methanol

    30-year discounted cash flow at 5 %, 0.3 % annual production degradation, with a 5,000-run Monte Carlo on electricity cost and prices and a one-at-a-time tornado analysis.

Limitations to keep in mind

  • The baseline solar yield is a clear-sky upper bound. With 2023 all-sky irradiance the same farm produces roughly a third less — use the simulator's basis switch to compare.
  • The plant is modelled as a constant load backed by the grid; electrolyser part-load behaviour, ramping, storage and hydrogen buffering are not modelled.
  • Hydrogen and methanol quantities on this page come from stoichiometry and an adjustable electrolyser consumption (default 50.9 kWh/kg H₂, which reproduces the project's 44,439 t/yr). Synthesis losses are not modelled.
  • The cost model's energy, export and import defaults are the project's fixed inputs and are independent of the hourly balance unless you link them to the scenario.
  • Scenario scaling is linear in capacity; a different turbine count would change wake losses in reality.
  • Re-running the notebook with current library versions gives a clear-sky solar yield about 0.1 % below the originally reported 582,338 MWh; the wind yield matches exactly.
